PROJECT CASE STUDY

Dream hacking to unlock creativity with Unblok

Unblok is a creative-block tool for iPhone and Apple Watch. It uses the edge of sleep to help you find ideas with a simple interface bound together by complex algorithms.

Role

Product Designer

Year

2025

— THE INSPIRATION

Dream hacking

We all have creative blocks, and it rarely helps by trying harder at creating, especially if you're burned out. The state that might help is the one we skip, hypnagogia (a really cool word indeed) - the moment before sleep when your thinking turns loose and associative.

In a 2023 study, people who napped with a guided dream topic scored 43% higher on creativity measures than people who napped without one. They scored 78% higher than people who stayed awake (MIT Media Lab). But it takes a lab and custom-built hardware, which includes a glove with various sensors, a computer, a pair of speakers and some open-source software that the researchers have released to control these interfaces.

Photo courtesy by MIT.

Then I had a question:
Could this work on devices people already own?

— CONCEPT

Interfacing dream with iOS & Apple Watch

Introducing Unblok —

Capturing when you fall into hypnagogia, speaking the prompts softly, and helping you get the idea you're trying to solve.

All with the help of science.

Set a session or choose a technique on iPhone

On iPhone, choose a session, or use/choose what you're stuck on or pick from the library prompts, such as "I need to find some ideas for my final solution about Irish health services" to sleep with it.

The watch reads your vitals, while prompt is spoken

Once a session starts, the Watch is synced and reads your heart rate, wrist tension, movements and breath rate in real time, somewhat same as the original Dormio glove used with no extra hardware.

During the session, the phone (or connected headphones) reads out and facilitates audio prompts based on the blockage subject for you, aiming to have that prompt stick around in your mind as you doze off. This will eventually influence what you think about subconsciously.

Idea capture upon waking

At the N2 sleep stage boundary, a gentle haptic pattern on the Watch wakes you before hypnagogia dissolves. You type or dictate in whatever comes on your mind at this very quick moment, before you forget about it, then your idea is saved and synced to your Journals on iPhone.

Sessions, insights and journal

Every capture lands in the Journal, time-stamped and tied to the session it came from. You can scroll back through past sessions in full, and machine learning predicts your sleep patterns better over time.

— RESEARCH, PROCESS, AND NERD STUFF

Behind the scenes

For Unblok, it was a dissertation project in my master's of design at Maynooth. I was in my own kind of creative block as I was stuck thinking about what to write my dissertation on, and trying to define the real problem that I needed to solve.

Normally, folks would be using using sticky notes and whiteboards (the only design thinking tool you need!), or attending workshops facilitated by someone else. Still, the most effective way is to have a conversation with someone to gain new perspectives.

But…what if we're alone?
Introverted?
Shy?
Working independently or as a freelancer?

It would be a little bit harder.

Comics by Sarah Andersen.

So that became my brief: build something simple, personal and useful for one individual.

The aha moment:
MIT's Dormio research

I came across MIT's Dormio - a sleep hacking study while doing the secondary research. Initially I thought of a safe path - a facilitated app that works like Headspace but for creativity, and you only need a phone (see scratchpad below).

The idea is inspired by how we sleep - during sleep onset, a strange introspective window arises in the form of hypnagogia, a semi-lucid sleep state where we all begin dreaming before we fall fully unconscious. Hypnagogia is characterized by experiential unpredictability, distorted perception of space and time, and spontaneous, fluid idea association.

Edison, Tesla, Poe, and Dalí each accessed this state by napping with a steel ball in hand to capture creative ideas generated in hypnagogic microdreams when it dropped to the floor below...

"Targeted dream incubation" concept by MIT

This uses sensory cues such as sounds, to steer dreams toward specific topics, followed by timed awakenings to record what the sleeper experienced.

Photo courtesy by MIT.

The challenge:

The difference from MIT's version is the hardware. Dormio reads muscle tone, heart rate and skin conductance through a glove.

I narrowed that to heart rate, breath and wrist motion, the three signals that Apple Watch gives reliably given today's latest hardware, and this is also the constraint, as it doesn't detect fingers muscle tension like Dormio does hence reduced accuracy.

Photo courtesy by MIT.

Feasible design decisions:

Sleep rules

One decision that I made was based on understandable sleep science: N1 stage triggers when heart rate drops by 5 bpm alongside 45s of stillness; N2 has an 8 bpm drop and 60s of stillness. And it's easy to capture that with the watch sensors.

One job per device.

The Apple Watch serves as the edge-detection trigger, signaling the bedside iPhone to play pre-recorded incubation prompts at a calibrated, gentle volume the moment physiological N1 thresholds are crossed.

Awakening

Combine Apple Watch haptics with ambient iPhone audio capture. Haptic vibration rouses the sleeper just enough to prompt recall and opens the microphone to record their verbal response.

Checking it with users

People said that they got at least one idea!

After building, I tested with 5 designer friends who owned the iPhone and Apple Watch pair.

3 woke up with at least one idea. 2 said they got good ones that solved their problem.

"It was like, I had to try several times…but then it worked like a charm. I was able to figure out what I was missing, part of me being refreshed and another thanking the app for letting me record it right away!"

— A test user.

Scratchpad ideas before the "Aha":
Unblok Facilitator

The other idea that I had before coming across dream incubation was to address a problem as the user uses AI to solve creative block, and most general chatbots are designed to agree with you and doesn't help you think critically and grow.

So, my former approach was building 'Putty' (as our mascot and the AI partner) with a creative technique library, and acts as a non-judgmental AI sparring partner designed to be a critic, asking you probing questions.

And the user can document their daily creative thoughts and ideas using the built in journal, both during or outside of sessions, as well as log their ideas as they spar with Putty.

Key takeaways

One thing I want to point out is that Unblok doesn't work every time. Success depends on many things we can't control, like how tired someone is, how fast they fall asleep and how well they remember a thought on waking. As we're human and sleep isn't a switch, Unblok aims to improve the odds of catching an idea.

This has been a very fun project for me to explore when working on my thesis and looking back, I really wish I had more time to experiment with more things and getting access to more immersive hardware for these experiences.

For anyone curious, I used Cursor & Xcode to build Unblok with SwiftUI!
