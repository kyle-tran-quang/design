PROJECT CASE STUDY

CarDoctor

Redesigning Vietnam's car service platform - turning a 70% drop-off rate into $1M in new revenue, and supporting it when it became backed by Vietnamese & Korean venture capitals.

Role

UX Designer

Year

2024 - 2025

Introduction

In Vietnam, vehicle maintenance is high-stress and low-trust: workshops operate in technical jargon, pricing is a hit or miss, and you don't know what happens when you hand them the car and your keys.
That's what CarDoctor aims to solve.

During two four-month contracts in 2024 and 2025, I worked at CarDoctor, a Vietnam-based automotive technology startup, supporting its growth as it secured VC funding, expanded its B2B network, and connected vehicle owners, workshops, and partners.

Who I worked with:

I worked together with one STO (single-threaded owner), 4 other designers, 3 PMs, 3 business analysts, and 12 developers on this project.

The initial problems

Initially, CarDoctor had a vendor/merchant service with B2B supply chains, and then it expanded with the purpose of connecting workshops and customers. The startup struggled to make revenue from their ecosystem from the consumer side. There were "a few" problems:

Churn rate:

70%

which is quite a lot, considering how users can't find quickly which good garage to go to for their car.

Missed opportunities

$50 - 100k

from user-side services, when the company was too focused on vendor relationships.

— PART 1

The "untrustworthy" UX

What and why

CarDoctor's premier consumer-facing product - the CarDoctor App, was weak in retaining customers.

Major audit from Google Analytics:

7 / 10 users

were dropping off from the home screen, hurting conversions and new customer growth due to "untrustworthy" design

There were many functions shown to the user upfront and broken flows. Also the second half of the homepage feels more like marketing than offering any real service.

At the same time, I talked to a few users since this would be the fastest way to uncover why they were dropping off. I was also surprised to learn that there were even more problems that we hadn't thought of, and that also included trust issues with the app itself.

I figured that the as-is product was focusing on selling repair services more than earning user trust first.

Your service is good, but I find myself confused [...] too many things and I don’t even know where to start, it’s too hard to find something I need.

→ Cognitive overload

I don’t know what’s going on with my car, only way is to ring the workshop [...] your chat feature didn’t help much.

→ Transparency & communication issue

PoV Statement

As a busy car owner, I need a reliable platform that offers a comprehensive list of trusted and verified mechanics and workshops, complete with user reviews and ratings. This way, I can make informed decisions and avoid the hassle of searching for reliable service providers.

— PART 2

How the app was revamped

Key features

For the first revamp, I worked on defining the key visuals for the brand, since its messy visuals and broken flows led to eroded trust.

Also, I focused on five forefront functionalities.
I've grouped these into 2 levels: building trust at first, and continuous customer service thereafter.

Search and Booking

Service tracking

Payment

Emergency Assistance

Maintenance guide

Level 1:
How do we build trust?

Search and Booking

Users had no structured way to compare workshops, and view prices upfront…

I introduced a location-first discovery flow featuring proximity, ratings, and services upfront. User research showed trust was the main barrier, and testing confirmed a search-first pattern created confusion.

Impact:

+38%

conversion from search to booking

Service Tracking

Users had no visibility into what was happening to their car after drop-off. This eroded trust for both sides. So the assumption is:

I designed the tracking flow to provides real-time updates on the milestones of the services. Users can monitor the progress of their vehicle's repairs or maintenance from start to finish.

Impact:

-42%

inbound calls from clients to workshops

Payment

In-app payment created anxiety for first-time users unfamiliar with trusting a new platform.

I decided to make a fee breakdown screen before any payment action, with itemized service cost, platform fee, total. Payment methods limited to the few most-used in Vietnam to avoid choice paralysis. The guarantee provided a peace of mind to customers, and boosted more in-app payments.

Impact:

+15%

in-app payment and reduced abandonment

Level 2:
How do we build lasting customer service?

Emergency Assistance

When cars broke down, owners' first instinct was to call a towing company. But, costs varied and availability was unpredictable.

I found that cost comparison was the critical differentiator, therefore when emergency assistance is needed, owners can quickly see the service proximity and cost before deciding.

Impact:

6.5 mins → <90 secs

time-to-dispatch for emergency breakdown services

Service history & maintenance guide

Owners usually had manual record of past services (service receipts) so a maintenance guide would be a "cherry on top" providing preventive maintenance insights for car owners.

This also acts as a record for their bookkeeping and providing customer information for each garage, so that any partner within the CarDoctor network would know about the history of their customers. Customers can also decide to share about their car conditions or not.

Impact:

+15%

retention rate for workshops, with reminder bookings

— PART 3

Addressing the gaps

After the major iteration I set out to gather stakeholder and user feedbacks. There were a few gaps that I was able to address, mostly to the business perspectives.

Real service tracking?

Due to the nature of the technician's work, they would follow all steps at once and they will not manually update each stages. Low-level works aren't updated in real time, so it can be hard for the customers to know.

I proposed that the app on the customer's side automatically estimates upcoming steps and notifies the client, to keep customers informed and reminds them of the services they ordered, while workshops simply mark tasks as "Done."

Some widgets that shows the car's status and other ongoing services to the client.

Commission bypass

This is the structurally largest and hardest one. Users find a garage on CarDoctor, then call them directly. The workshop has every incentive to encourage this and it cuts the commission. Unlike an on-demand service like Uber, the client usually doesn't have urgency and there are many choices.

Also, culturally people like to pay after the service has been done, directly to the workshop.

I raised this concern and addressed this as a "revenue leak" and came up with a hypothesis before I left - so this is still an open problem.

— PART 4

Outcome

Reception

CarDoctor achieved good retention rates, reflecting the app's ability to meet user needs effectively, which contributed to a $1M revenue increase by the end of 2025.

$1 million

revenue from SaaS, vendor supply chain, assistance services, and in-app services.

In 2025, the company got VC-backed by Noble Edge Effect and Titans Ventures, and expanded to other cities in Vietnam.

Challenges

One of the primary challenges was building user trust as a new platform, convincing owners to switch from their traditional methods of finding mechanics to using an app.

We tackled this by giving out promotions to try out the services, with free vehicle inspection packages aimed at garages, with some cost involved. The service guarantees we offered eventually built trust with customers, with a 87% satisfaction rate.

Some promotional images for our offerings. One - "Easy and convenience service warranty" and Two - "Boost customer returns", aimed at customers and workshops.

Lessons learned

Design for trust

Early user testing showed that better search wouldn't help if users didn't believe the platform was reliable. We shifted our first sprint to focus entirely on credibility signals before discovery.

Creativity within constraints

I found that the limitations we faced: technical, budget, or time - these tradeoffs really pushed me to think outside of the box and stick to what's important.

Design changes as startup grows

Lots of iterations were made. A startup’s needs can change quickly, so we need to stay flexible and adapt.
