PROJECT CASE STUDY

CarDoctor Design System, redone in 4 months

A complete redesign of the CarDoctor system — achieving 2x faster development time and a clear, cohesive look and feel across the brand presence.

Role

UX Designer

Year

2024

How's it possible?

I was working closely with a team of 4 designers and 4 frontend devs, we built a feedback loop that balanced creativity with technical feasibility.

Nothing is ever perfect, so we applied the “steal like an artist” philosophy that borrows design patterns and component logic from frameworks like Tailwind CSS & ShadCDN to create a clean, efficient, and scalable design system (currently under NDA so shhh… I can only share this much).

Challenge

CarDoctor’s design system was inconsistent, culturally mismatched, and lacked ownership.

Approach & impact

I led a four-month redesign to build a unified, locally aligned system.

The impact was 2x faster development, stronger cross-team alignment, and a modern, cohesive user experience that sets the tone for future CarDoctor projects.

— PART 1

The need for tidying up

Backstory

When I joined CarDoctor, I suddenly found myself wearing every hat - strategist, senior, and decision-maker.

At the time, everything was a patchwork of visual trends, copying popular fintech apps in Vietnam without truly understanding user needs and just ticking off stakeholder requests.

Audit

One of the biggest challenges I faced while designing CarDoctor’s platform was the growing inconsistency between the design and the live product. Something felt off - were the developers working with a different set of assets? Nope.

Problem 1:
Inconsistent styles and elements


The system had disorganized variants of the same elements: buttons, icons and unpolished color theory, leading to a confusing experience that made people drop off from the home screen.

Problem 2:
Misalignment with local context


Instead of building a dedicated system, the design team had borrowed components directly from a Korean product design system.

Laziness didn't pay off well as it was a nightmare for developers.

Problem 3:
No clear guidelines or ownership


There were no guiding principles. Below is everything we had at the moment.

— PART 2

Building the simple design system

Visual and style guidelines

I began by defining a type scale that improved readability across all screens, introduced a balanced color system for contrast and accessibility, and established consistent grid styles and iconography. Color semantics across tokens were also laid out.

Buttons, spacing and labelling

We categorized every existing button, found the patterns that actually worked, and rebuilt them into a single, flexible button system with clear spacing, labelling rules and responsive states.

Tokens and Variables

In early 2024, I stumbled across Figma’s new Variables feature, and felt that it would really be helpful. That was exactly what we needed to upscale our system.

Before that, updating colors or typography meant manual changes across countless components.

We implemented a token-based structure that defined tokens for context that automatically cascaded through every component.
